Offline Games for Kids on iPad: 5 Apps Compared for Road Trips and Flights

Comparisons By Zach · 2 November 2026 · 8 min read

Looking for offline games for kids on iPad before a long drive or a flight? The catch is that “works offline” can mean very different things: some apps have everything built in, others only work after you’ve downloaded each game at home. I’m a dad who makes a calm driving game, so I compared four popular kids’ apps with mine for ages 6–8, using what each developer and App Store page says.

Key takeaways

  • Shape Valley and GameForKidz have every game built into a small download; Sago Mini World and Toca Boca Jr need you to download games before you travel.
  • Pok Pok works offline after you download it and subscribe once, but asks you to reconnect every few weeks for updates.
  • GameForKidz is the best value for variety: 97 games, 28 free, one $9.99 unlock. Sago Mini World and Pok Pok are lovely for younger siblings.
  • Shape Valley is a calm, no-race driving game made for ages 6–8. It’s one type of game, and the free plan is limited.

On this page

What “offline” really means for kids’ apps

Apple’s editors ran a feature on kids’ apps for offline fun, and the useful detail is the small print: each app handles offline play differently. I check four things:

  • Built in or download-first? Some apps ship every game in the download. Bigger “world” apps make you save games for offline play at home.
  • Subscriptions. Buying, restoring or updating needs a connection.
  • Ads and accounts. Fewer interruptions, fewer sign-in surprises.
  • Who it’s really for. Two of the “2–8” apps here sit in Apple’s 0–5 Kids band, which matters if your child is 7.

No single winner: two small built-in apps, one calm playroom and two huge libraries for younger kids.

GameForKidz

GameForKidz takes the games from its free website and puts all 97 of them into an app for children aged 3–12. The developer says every game works offline, with no ads, no tracking, no sign-in and no subscription: 28 games are free, and a single $9.99 in-app purchase unlocks the other 69. The App Store privacy label says the developer collects no data. It has a daily time limit and a calm mode with less motion.

It’s the best value here, with the widest age range. But it’s a collection of puzzles, classics and arcade-style games, most with levels, not one calm world.

Good for

  • Long flights where variety matters
  • Siblings of different ages (3–12)
  • Parents who want to pay once, never a subscription

Worth knowing

  • Includes arcade-style games, so pick calm ones for wind-down
  • Levels and stars may appeal more to some kids than others
  • A newer app, so fewer reviews to go on

Pok Pok

Pok Pok is a low-stimulation, Montessori-inspired playroom of digital toys, from a town and a house to a marble machine and drawing. There’s no winning or losing, no language and no ads. Its FAQ says you only need the internet to download the app and subscribe once, then it works offline. It asks you to reconnect every few weeks for updates.

It’s a subscription with a 7-day free trial. The US App Store lists monthly plans from $6.99, annual plans and a $89.99 lifetime option. The listing says ages 2–8, the FAQ says 2–7, and Apple lists it as Made for Ages 0–5.

Good for

  • Calm, open-ended play with nothing to lose
  • Younger siblings and gentle 6-year-olds
  • Families who value quiet sound

Worth knowing

  • Subscription, though a lifetime option exists
  • Older kids may want more of a goal
  • About 1 GB to download

Sago Mini World

Sago Mini World is a big, cheerful world of preschool games, part of Piknik’s subscription bundle. The listing says kids can play downloaded games offline. Its help article explains: tap the Parents icon, open Games and download the ones you want before you lose signal. Only some come with the base app.

Subscribers get no ads or in-app purchases; US plans start at $7.99 after a free trial. The developer says ages 2–6 and Apple lists it as Made for Ages 0–5, so most 7 and 8 year olds will find it young.

Good for

  • Younger siblings (2–5)
  • A huge range of gentle activities

Worth knowing

  • Offline only for games you’ve downloaded
  • Aimed at preschoolers
  • About 937 MB before extra games

Toca Boca Jr

Toca Boca Jr bundles Toca Boca favourites such as Kitchen 2, Pet Doctor, Builders and Cars into one subscription app, also from Piknik. Like Sago Mini World, it plays downloaded games offline. Piknik’s help page is clear that you need a connection to download each game first. There’s no third-party advertising and, per the listing, no in-app purchases beyond the subscription.

The listing says ages 2–8; Apple lists it as Made for Ages 0–5. At about 1.9 GB, it’s the biggest app here.

Good for

  • Kids who love pretend play: cooking, pets, building
  • Lots of variety in one place

Worth knowing

  • Download-first offline play
  • Large download and storage needs
  • Livelier than the calm apps here

Shape Valley

Shape Valley is the calm driving game I built for my son, who found busy games overwhelming. It’s made for ages 6–8, and all five games are inside the 11 MB download: Calm Drive, Taxi, Tidy Dozer, Lawnmower and the new Vacuum Bot. Nothing to pre-download, no account and no ads or tracking in the app. Only buying or restoring a subscription needs the internet.

Its limits: it’s one kind of game, with far less variety than GameForKidz or the Piknik apps. The free Basic plan includes Calm Drive with sessions of up to five minutes. The Family plan unlocks every game with no session limit: $4.99 a month, $29.99 a year after a 7-day free trial, or $59.99 once. It’s brand new, and it’s for iPhone and iPad only (no Android).

Good for

  • Kids aged 6–8 who love driving but not racing
  • Trips where you want calm, not hype
  • Parents who want no account and nothing to pre-download

Worth knowing

  • One game type, a smaller library
  • The free plan is short (Calm Drive, 5-minute sessions)
  • No Android version

Pre-trip checklist

  1. Download at home, on Wi-Fi. Open each app and save the games you want. A big update or low storage can remove downloaded games, so update first, then download.
  2. Test in Airplane Mode. Switch it on the night before and open every app your child might ask for.
  3. Charge everything and pack headphones.
  4. Set limits in advance. Screen Time or Guided Access (which can lock the iPad to one app with a time limit) saves negotiating mid-flight.

Car sickness: if your child gets travel sick, screens may not be the answer. The NHS advises against reading, watching films or using devices if you get motion sickness, and suggests talking, music or singing instead. Save the iPad for the plane or rest stops.

Frequently asked questions

Do kids’ apps work in Airplane Mode?

Many do, in different ways. Shape Valley and GameForKidz have every game built in; Pok Pok works after you download and subscribe; Sago Mini World and Toca Boca Jr only play games you’ve downloaded. Test in Airplane Mode before you travel.

Do subscription apps work offline?

Generally yes, once the subscription is active. Buying or restoring needs a connection, and Pok Pok asks you to reconnect every few weeks. Open them online before you leave.

What are the best offline iPad games for a long flight?

For variety, GameForKidz (97 built-in games, one optional $9.99 unlock). For younger siblings, Sago Mini World or Pok Pok. For a calm, no-race game for ages 6–8, Shape Valley.

Which offline games suit kids who get car sick?

The NHS advises against using devices if you’re prone to motion sickness and suggests talking, music or singing instead. Save screens for flights or breaks.

Does Shape Valley need the internet?

No. All five games work fully offline; only buying or restoring a subscription needs a connection. No accounts, no ads.
