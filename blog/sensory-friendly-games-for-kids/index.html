Sensory-Friendly Games for Kids: Shape Valley vs Pok Pok vs Sago Mini World vs Toca Boca World

Comparisons By Zach · 9 November 2026 · 8 min read

If loud, flashy apps frazzle your child, you’ve probably searched for sensory friendly games for kids and found that every app claims to be calm. My son is one of those kids: busy games like Mario Kart left him overwhelmed, which is why I built my own calm driving game. So, full disclosure, Shape Valley is mine. Here I compare it fairly with Pok Pok, Sago Mini World and Toca Boca World on how gentle they really are for sensitive kids aged 6–8, using their App Store pages and an independent calm rating.

Key takeaways

  • Pok Pok is the calmest all-rounder here: it describes itself as low-stimulation and sensory-friendly, with no ads and no winning or losing, for ages 2–8.
  • Sago Mini World is gentle and great for 2–5 year olds, but busier on screen and young for most 7 and 8 year olds.
  • Toca Boca World is a brilliant creative sandbox for ages 6–8, but it’s the busiest of the four, with lots happening at once.
  • Shape Valley is one calm, focused activity: driving with no racing and no ads, made for ages 6–8. It has far less variety than the others.

What makes an app sensory-friendly?

The Child Mind Institute explains that some children are hypersensitive: things feel louder and brighter to them than to other people, and they can get overwhelmed easily. Bright lights and loud noises top their list of struggles. Many of these kids have no diagnosis at all.

For apps, that turns into a short checklist. It’s the one I use, and it’s a parent’s checklist, not a lab test:

  • Sound: is it soft and occasional, or constant music with sudden effects?
  • Motion: does the screen sit still until your child acts, or is something always moving?
  • Rewards and pop-ups: coin showers, stars, unlock prompts and shop buttons all add up.
  • Pace and pressure: timers, scores and losing raise the stakes for a child who already feels a lot.
  • Age fit: too young bores; too complex frustrates.

If you want a more formal measure, Harmless App publishes a 1–10 Calm Score for toddler apps, based on sounds, animations, rewards and brightness. I’ve included their scores where they exist, with credit.

Pok Pok

Pok Pok is a Montessori-inspired collection of digital toys, from a town and a house to blocks and a marble machine. Its App Store page calls it low-stimulation and says the experience is “calming and sensory-friendly”, with soft, hand-recorded sounds and slow-paced animations. There are no ads, no language and no winning or losing. It has won an Apple Design Award.

It’s a subscription after a free trial; the US listing shows monthly and annual plans plus a $89.99 lifetime option. The developer says ages 2–8; Apple places it in its 0–5 Kids band, and some older kids may want more of a goal.

Good for

  • Sensitive kids who love open-ended, exploratory play
  • Families with siblings from 2 upwards
  • Parents who want the most proven calm app

Worth knowing

  • Subscription, though a lifetime option exists
  • No goals, which some 7–8 year olds may miss
  • About 1 GB download

Sago Mini World

Sago Mini World is a huge, cheerful world of preschool activities, from baking to dinosaurs, and part of Piknik’s subscription bundle. The listing promises no ads or in-app purchases for subscribers and says it’s for kids aged 2–6; Apple lists it as Made for Ages 0–5. Harmless App describes it as gentle play with soft characters and no points or pressure, though its measured Calm Score is lower than Pok Pok’s.

Good for

  • Younger siblings aged 2–5
  • Lots of gentle variety in one app

Worth knowing

  • Young for most 7 and 8 year olds
  • Lower independent Calm Score than Pok Pok
  • Subscription after the free trial

Toca Boca World

Toca Boca World is a vast open-world dollhouse: kids build homes, design characters, run cafés and make up stories. Credit where it’s due: it’s Made for Ages 6–8 on the App Store, has no third-party ads, no time limits or high scores, and is wonderful for creative kids. It’s free to download, with optional one-time packs in its shop.

For a sensitive child, though, it’s the busiest app here. Harmless App’s estimate puts it lowest of the four, describing an enormous sandbox with lots happening at once. Many kids love that; some find it a lot.

Good for

  • Creative 6–8 year olds who like lots to do
  • Storytelling and pretend play
  • Free to start

Worth knowing

  • Rated busiest of the four (Harmless App estimate)
  • Shop and packs to manage
  • Big app, about 1 GB

Shape Valley

Shape Valley is the game I made for my son, and for kids like him who want “car games” without the overwhelm. It’s made for ages 6–8 and built from soft, simple shapes in warm colours; nothing flashes, beeps or rushes. Kids just steer while the car rolls on its own, and games end with a kind message, never “game over”.

There are five games: Calm Drive (wander with no goals or timer), Taxi (glowing rings guide passengers home), Tidy Dozer (push toys into a hole), Lawnmower (mow tall grass into stripes) and the new Vacuum Bot. There’s no racing, no ads and no account, and purchases sit behind a parental gate.

Where Shape Valley is honestly better

  • One focused activity instead of many busy scenes, which can make it easier to stop.
  • A driving game with no racing, no “game over” and nothing rushing your child, which is rare.
  • Built for 6–8 year olds, rather than a preschool app.
  • No ads, no shop, no accounts: a single Family unlock behind a parental gate.

Where it isn’t

  • It’s one type of game with a small library, not open-ended creative play like Pok Pok or Toca Boca World.
  • It isn’t made for under-5s.
  • The free plan is short: Calm Drive only, in sessions of up to five minutes.
  • It’s brand new with few ratings, and it needs iOS 18 or later (the others support older devices).

If racing games are the sticking point at your house, my guide to racing games for sensitive kids goes deeper, and the low-stimulation games guide covers what to look for more broadly.

Tips for playing with a sensitive child

  • Turn the volume down, or use headphones set low.
  • Silence notifications so banners and sounds don’t pop up mid-game. Guided Access can also lock the iPad to one app with a time limit.
  • Keep sessions short and warn before ending. See my guide to ending screen time without a meltdown.
  • Pick one app per session, not several.
  • Watch your child, not the label. If they come away wound up, it’s too much for them, whatever the store says. My guide to the signs of an overstimulated child can help you spot it.

Frequently asked questions

What makes an app sensory-friendly?

Soft, occasional sound, little unprompted motion, no flashing rewards or pop-ups, no timers or pressure, and no ads. A calm app waits for your child rather than constantly grabbing their attention.

Are these apps good for autistic kids?

Many families of autistic children like calm apps such as Pok Pok, and Apple has featured Pok Pok in a collection of apps to support children with autism. But every child is different, and no app is a treatment. Watch how your own child responds.

Which sensory-friendly app is the calmest?

Of these four, Pok Pok has the highest independent Calm Score from Harmless App (6.6 out of 10). Shape Valley is designed to be very calm too but hasn’t been rated by Harmless App. Toca Boca World is the busiest.

Do these kids’ apps have ads?

No third-party ads in any of the four, per their App Store pages. Sago Mini World is ad-free for subscribers; Toca Boca World has no third-party ads but does have an in-app shop.
