Car Games for Kids on iPad: 5 Driving Apps Compared Fairly

Comparisons By Zach · 26 October 2026 · 8 min read

If you’re looking for car games for kids on iPad, the App Store is full of them, and they are not all alike. Some are loud racers with subscriptions, some are gentle building toys, and a few are made for winding down. I’m a dad who builds a calm driving game, so I looked closely at four popular kids’ car apps and put them side by side with mine, for ages 6–8, as fairly as I can.

Key takeaways

  • Four popular kids’ car apps compared with Shape Valley on price, ads, in-app purchases, platforms and pace.
  • Fiete Cars and Labo Brick Car 2 are creative build-and-drive toys; Sago Mini Trips+ is gentle but aimed at ages 2–5.
  • Hot Wheels Unlimited is a fast, fun racer with a subscription, and its Google Play listing says it contains ads.
  • Shape Valley is a slow, no-race driving game for ages 6–8. It isn’t on the App Store yet.

On this page

What I looked at (and why)

Search “car game” and you get everything from toddler toys to full-throttle racers, nearly all rated 4+. So I checked the same things for each app:

  • Who it’s made for. The App Store shows an age rating (4+) and, for apps in the Kids category, an age band such as “Made for Ages 6–8”. Those are different things.
  • Price and in-app purchases. Free to download often means a paid unlock or a subscription inside.
  • Ads and platforms. Even “rewarded” ads interrupt play; Android matters for mixed households.
  • Pace. Racing, timers and power-ups versus slow, open-ended driving.

There’s no single “best” car game: two creative toys, a gentle app for younger kids, an exciting racer and one deliberately slow game (mine). It depends on your child.

Fiete Cars – KidsKlub

Fiete Cars from Ahoiii is a build-your-own car world. Kids lay down roads, ramps and obstacles, pick from 40 vehicles (including a taxi, a tractor and, delightfully, a horse) and then drive through what they built. The App Store listing says kids can choose between “high-speed stunts, adventurous chases or leisurely drives”, which is a fair summary: it can be calm or lively depending on the child.

It’s free to download. On the US App Store the in-app purchases are a $17.99 “Unlock all” full version and KidsKlub memberships from $6.99 a month. The developer says all KidsKlub apps are ad-free, and the listing shows a Parents’ Area with in-app controls.

Good for

  • Creative kids who like building as much as driving
  • Kids who enjoy silly vehicles and making up stories
  • Families happy to pay once to unlock everything

Worth knowing

  • Ramps, jumps and stunts can make play lively
  • The KidsKlub subscription is offered alongside the one-off unlock, so check what you’re buying
  • iPhone and iPad; I couldn’t confirm an Android version

Labo Brick Car 2

Labo Brick Car 2 lets kids build cars, trucks, buses and even tanks from virtual bricks, using more than 140 templates or free building, and then drive them through levels and mini-games. Building is absorbing.

It’s free with a single $3.99 in-app purchase for the full version on the US App Store, which is good value. Labo Lado says it doesn’t collect personal information or include third-party advertising in its apps. It’s also on Google Play. The App Store category is Racing, though, and the levels include racing, so the driving part is more energetic than the building part.

Good for

  • Kids who love building and designing vehicles
  • Mixed iPad and Android households
  • A low, one-off price

Worth knowing

  • The driving levels lean towards racing
  • There’s an online sharing feature for browsing other players’ cars
  • Last App Store update listed in 2024

Sago Mini Trips+

Sago Mini Trips+ bundles four Sago Mini travel games, including Road Trip, into one Apple Arcade app. You pack a suitcase, pick a silly vehicle and drive to a destination, with stops for a car wash or fuel along the way. Sago Mini describes it as calming and frustration-free, with no time limits or rules, and it works without Wi-Fi.

It’s part of Apple Arcade, which costs $6.99 a month or $49.99 a year in the US and can be shared with family. There are no ads or in-app purchases. The one thing to know for this age group: the developer says it’s perfect for kids aged 2–5, so many 6–8 year olds will find it too young.

Good for

  • Younger siblings, or a 6-year-old who still loves gentle pretend play
  • Car trips and flights (no internet needed)
  • Families who already have Apple Arcade

Worth knowing

  • Designed for ages 2–5, so older kids may outgrow it quickly
  • Needs an Apple Arcade subscription
  • More of a road-trip toy than a driving game

Hot Wheels Unlimited

Hot Wheels Unlimited by Budge Studios is the most exciting app here, and it doesn’t pretend otherwise: build huge stunt tracks with loops and jumps, race against rivals, use power-ups and collect famous Hot Wheels cars. It’s listed as Made for Ages 6–8, and lots of kids that age love it.

It’s free to download, with membership plans from $12.99 a month on the US App Store. Its Google Play listing is labelled “Contains ads” and in-app purchases. Budge says it holds the ESRB Privacy Certified Kids’ Privacy Seal.

Good for

  • Kids who want speed, stunts and something to win
  • Hot Wheels fans and track-building fans
  • Two-player races with a sibling or friend

Worth knowing

  • Fast, busy racing can be a lot for sensitive kids
  • Subscriptions and ads are part of the free version
  • A large download (about 1.2 GB on iOS)

Shape Valley

Shape Valley is the calm driving game I built for my son, who loved cars but found racing games overwhelming. It’s designed for kids aged 6–8 and has four gentle modes: Calm Drive, Taxi, Tidy Dozer and Lawnmower. There’s no race, no timer, no scores and no “game over”.

There are no ads and no tracking in the app, and all four modes work offline. In the Parent Zone you can set play time, driving speed and time of day, and auto drive helps kids who just want to ride along. There’s a free Basic plan and a Family plan with a 7-day free trial, then $29.99 a year.

Good for

  • Kids aged 6–8 who love driving but not racing
  • Winding down after school or before bed
  • Car journeys and flights (works offline)

Worth knowing

  • Deliberately slow, so thrill-seekers may prefer Hot Wheels
  • No track building like Fiete Cars or Labo
  • iPhone and iPad only, and not on the App Store yet

Which car game suits your child?

  • Loves building and tinkering? Try Fiete Cars or Labo Brick Car 2. Both reward creativity, and Labo is the cheapest full unlock here.
  • Younger sibling, or still loves gentle pretend play? Sago Mini Trips+ is lovely if you already have Apple Arcade.
  • Wants speed and something to win? Hot Wheels Unlimited delivers, but keep an eye on subscriptions and ads.
  • Gets overwhelmed or upset by racing? A slow, no-race game like Shape Valley is built for exactly that. My guides to racing games for sensitive kids and non-competitive driving games go deeper.

Tip: Play any new game together for the first ten minutes to see whether your child comes away calm or wound up. The American Academy of Pediatrics suggests making a family media plan so screen time has clear limits everyone knows in advance.

Frequently asked questions

What are good car games for kids on iPad?

It depends on the child: Fiete Cars or Labo Brick Car 2 for builders, Sago Mini Trips+ for younger kids, Hot Wheels Unlimited for racing fans, and Shape Valley for slow, no-race driving at ages 6–8.

Which kids’ car games have no ads?

Per the developers, Fiete Cars, Labo Brick Car 2 (no third-party ads), Sago Mini Trips+ and Shape Valley have no ads. Hot Wheels Unlimited’s Google Play listing says it contains ads.

What age is Shape Valley for?

Shape Valley is designed for kids aged 6 to 8. Auto drive and big, simple controls help younger players, and older kids can steer on their own.

How much does Shape Valley cost?

There’s a free Basic plan. The Family plan has a 7-day free trial, then costs $29.99 a year.
